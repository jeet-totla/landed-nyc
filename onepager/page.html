The problem

In June 2025 the FARE Act made landlords pay their own brokers. A year later renters still pay fees nobody can explain, and nobody checks your paperwork before you pay. It hits newcomers hardest: students and workers arriving from abroad have no US credit history, no local guarantor and no way to tell a legal fee from an illegal one. 137,799 international students rent in New York State alone.

I moved to New York this term with TETR, which relocates students to a new country every semester, so this is close to home.

The solution

Landed checks a lease, listing or renewal offer against New York law and cites the rule behind every flag. Rules decide. AI only explains and drafts the reply. Ten tools cover two moments:

Before you sign

  • Lease and listing checker
  • Landlord and broker vetting (live HPD data)
  • Move-in cost with no credit
  • Renter passport, stabilization screen, condition log

After you've signed

  • Renewal check
  • Fee audit and complaint draft
  • Heat and hot water, 311 draft
  • Deposit clock and demand letter

The sample lease on the home page carries $9,650 in overcharges.

Why it's different: listing sites show apartments and legal clinics help after a dispute. As far as I can tell, nothing checks your paperwork against the law before you pay, with a source on every flag, and works for renters who have no US credit.

The business (hypotheses to test)

How it grows

Now: free for every renter, to build trust. Later: core checks stay free; a few extra services cost a very small amount.

Who could pay or partner

  • Universities and relocation programs for arriving international students.
  • Employers that bring in many interns and new hires and need to sort out their housing.
  • Landlords and brokers who want to show a listing follows the FARE Act.
  • Paid low-price services, such as a renter pack of filings and documents.
  • Government: become a trusted site the city promotes and works with.

Why it's credible

One caught fee is worth more than any price: no broker fee saves $5,454 upfront on average (StreetEasy via Hoodline). Rules are fixed and data is open, so it costs little to run. Next test: a pilot with one student cohort.

Process, AI toolkit and my part

Process. Spotted the problem in reporting and the law. Checked every rule and number against statutes and agency pages (dated in docs/RULES.md). Built a tested rules engine with live HPD and licence lookups, then tested on desktop and phone.
